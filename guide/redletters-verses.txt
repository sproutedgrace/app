# Red Letters context guide: pop-up notes on verses that are easy to misread.
# Format: Book Chapter:Verse | Note
Matthew 5:17 | Jesus says He came to fulfill the Law and the Prophets, not to wipe them out. Christians understand "fulfill" in different ways. Read it with the rest of the chapter, where He goes deeper than the rules, to the heart.
Matthew 5:29 | This is a strong word-picture, a common way to teach in Jesus' time. He means: take sin seriously and cut off what leads you into it. He is not telling anyone to hurt their body.
Matthew 5:39 | A slap on the right cheek with the back of the hand was an insult, not an attack. Jesus is teaching His followers not to trade insult for insult.
Matthew 6:6 | Jesus is not against praying with others. He is warning against praying to look good in front of people.
Matthew 7:1 | This does not mean "never tell right from wrong." Keep reading: take the beam out of your own eye first, then you can see clearly to help your brother.
Matthew 7:6 | In Jesus' day, dogs and pigs were not pets. The point is to use wisdom about when and with whom you share holy things. It is not a name to call people.
Matthew 10:34 | The "sword" is a picture of division. Keep reading: following Jesus could split families. He is not telling anyone to fight. In Luke 12:51 the same saying says "division."
Matthew 11:12 | This verse is hard to translate, and Bibles word it in different ways. It is not about using force to get into heaven.
Matthew 12:31 | Leaders had just said Jesus' good works came from the devil. This warning is about a heart that calls God's good work evil and refuses to turn. Someone worried they have done it usually has not.
Matthew 15:26 | "Dogs" was a word some Jewish people used for outsiders. Jesus' words test her, and her reply wins. He praises her great faith and heals her daughter.
Matthew 16:18 | "Peter" means "rock." Christians read "this rock" in different ways: Peter himself, Peter's faith, or Jesus. All agree the church belongs to Jesus.
Matthew 16:28 | Christians read this in different ways. Many tie it to the next chapter, where three followers see Jesus shine bright on the mountain.
Matthew 19:24 | A camel was the biggest animal around and a needle's eye the smallest hole. The picture is meant to be impossible. Keep reading: "with God all things are possible."
Matthew 22:21 | The coin had Caesar's picture on it. People are made in God's image, so the deeper point is: give yourself to God.
Matthew 23:3 | Jesus tells people to follow what the leaders teach from Moses, but not to copy how they live. The whole chapter is about leaders who did not practice what they preached.
Matthew 24:34 | Christians read "this generation" in different ways. Some tie it to the temple falling in AD 70. Others tie it to the last days. Jesus' main point is to be ready.
Matthew 26:11 | Jesus is not saying the poor don't matter. He is quoting Deuteronomy 15:11, which goes on to say "open thine hand wide" to the poor.
Matthew 26:52 | Peter had just cut off a servant's ear to defend Jesus. Jesus tells him to put the sword away, and in Luke He heals the ear.
Matthew 27:46 | This is the first line of Psalm 22. That Psalm begins in pain and ends in hope and praise. Many people in Jesus' day would have known how it ends.
Mark 4:11 | Verse 12 quotes Isaiah 6. It describes hearts that do not want to hear. Parables open up to people who come close and ask, like the followers did.
Mark 7:27 | See the note on Matthew 15:26. Read to the end: the woman's answer wins, and her daughter is healed.
Mark 9:43 | Like Matthew 5:29, this is a strong word-picture: take sin seriously. Jesus is not telling anyone to hurt their body.
Mark 10:25 | See the note on Matthew 19:24. The point: no one can save themselves, but God can.
Mark 11:23 | "This mountain" may point to the temple mount they were standing near. The lesson is to trust God, not to use faith to get anything you want.
Mark 13:32 | Jesus says no one knows the day or the hour. Be careful with anyone who claims they do.
Mark 16:17 | Verses 9 to 20 are not in the oldest copies of Mark. Please do not handle snakes or drink poison to test your faith. Jesus also said, "Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God."
Luke 9:60 | This may mean the man's father was still alive, and he wanted to wait years. Jesus is saying: following Me can't wait.
Luke 12:51 | Following Jesus can cause division, even in families. Jesus is describing what may happen, not telling anyone to cause it.
Luke 14:26 | In Jesus' language, "hate" in this kind of saying means "love less." Matthew 10:37 says it plainly: love Jesus more than family. Jesus also taught to honor your father and mother.
Luke 16:9 | This puzzling verse is about using money wisely to bless people, so it serves what lasts. It is not praise for cheating.
Luke 17:21 | "Within you" can also be read "among you" or "in your midst." Jesus was standing right there. Both readings are found in Bibles.
Luke 18:22 | This was said to one man whose money came first in his heart. Jesus did not ask everyone to sell everything, but He calls everyone to put God first.
Luke 22:36 | Read on to verse 38. When the followers show two swords, Jesus says "It is enough." Hours later, He stops Peter from using one. Many see this as a word-picture about hard times ahead.
Luke 23:43 | The first Bibles had no commas, so some read it "I say unto thee today, thou shalt be with me." Christians read it both ways.
John 2:4 | "Woman" was a polite way to speak then, like "ma'am." Jesus helped anyway.
John 3:5 | Christians read "born of water and of the Spirit" in different ways. Some think of baptism, some of natural birth, some of being made clean inside.
John 4:22 | Jesus is speaking to a Samaritan about where God's promises came through, the people of Israel. In verse 23 He says true worship is "in spirit and in truth," anywhere.
John 6:53 | This shocked people then too, and many walked away. Christians understand it in different ways, often linked to the Lord's Supper. In verse 63 Jesus says, "the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit."
John 8:44 | This was said in one heated argument with certain leaders who wanted to kill Him. It is not about all Jewish people. Jesus and all His first followers were Jewish.
John 10:34 | Jesus quotes Psalm 82:6. He was answering leaders who said He blasphemed by calling Himself God's Son. It does not mean people become God.
John 14:6 | Thomas had just asked, "How can we know the way?" This is Jesus' answer to His friends the night before He died.
John 14:12 | "Greater works" may mean more works, spread farther, by many people after Jesus goes to the Father.
John 15:6 | This is a picture from vineyards, where dead branches were burned. The main point is in verse 5: stay close to Him and you will bear fruit.
John 20:23 | Christians understand this in different ways. Jesus had just given His followers the Holy Spirit and sent them out to share forgiveness.
Acts 1:7 | Jesus' last word on timing: it's not for us to know. He points them to their job instead, being witnesses.
Acts 9:4 | Saul was hurting Jesus' followers. Jesus asks why Saul is hurting Him. What we do to His people, we do to Him.
Revelation 2:9 | This is about certain people in that one city who were troubling the church. It must never be used against Jewish people.
Revelation 3:9 | Like 2:9, this is about one local group in John's time. It is not about Jewish people today.
Revelation 3:16 | Laodicea's water was lukewarm, not hot like the springs nearby or cold like fresh water. The church was neither healing nor refreshing anyone.
Revelation 22:18 | "This book" means the book of Revelation. John is warning people not to change what he wrote.
