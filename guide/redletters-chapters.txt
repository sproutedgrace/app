# Red Letters context guide: one note per chapter.
# Format: Book Chapter | Where and who | What to keep in mind
Matthew 3 | By the Jordan River. Jesus comes to John the Baptist to be baptized. | Jesus' first words in Matthew. John feels he should be baptized by Jesus, not the other way around. Jesus says it is right to do it anyway.
Matthew 4 | The wilderness, then Galilee. Jesus is tested for 40 days, then calls His first followers. | Each time He is tested, Jesus answers with Scripture from Deuteronomy. "Fishers of men" was said to real fishermen, in words they knew.
Matthew 5 | A hillside in Galilee. Jesus teaches His followers while a crowd listens. This begins the Sermon on the Mount (chapters 5 to 7). | Jesus often says "Ye have heard... but I say unto you." He is going deeper than the rules, to the heart. Some lines are strong word-pictures meant to wake people up.
Matthew 6 | Still the Sermon on the Mount. | Jesus talks about giving, praying, fasting, money, and worry. The big idea: do good for God to see, not for people to see.
Matthew 7 | The end of the Sermon on the Mount. | "Judge not" is about looking down on others while ignoring your own faults. The sermon ends with a choice: build your life on what He said, or on sand.
Matthew 8 | Coming down the hill and around the Sea of Galilee. Jesus heals and calms a storm. | A Roman officer, an outsider, shows great faith. Jesus praises him. Faith is not limited to one group.
Matthew 9 | Capernaum, Jesus' home base by the lake. | Religious leaders complain that Jesus eats with tax collectors and sinners. He answers that the sick are the ones who need a doctor.
Matthew 10 | Jesus sends out His 12 followers to teach and heal. | These instructions were for that first trip, to Israel only. Later (Matthew 28) He sends them to all nations. Hard sayings here warn that following Him may cost something.
Matthew 11 | Messengers come from John the Baptist, who is in prison. | John wonders if Jesus is really the One. Jesus answers by pointing to what is happening: the blind see, the poor hear good news. Then He invites the tired to rest.
Matthew 12 | Grain fields and a synagogue on the Sabbath. Pharisees, a strict religious group, argue with Jesus. | The arguments are about what is allowed on the Sabbath. Jesus says helping people is right on the Sabbath. Strong words in this chapter answer people who said His good works came from the devil.
Matthew 13 | From a boat by the lake, Jesus tells parables to a crowd on the shore. | Parables are short stories with a lesson. Jesus explains some of them Himself, like the Sower. The "kingdom of heaven" stories show small beginnings that grow big.
Matthew 14 | After John the Baptist is killed. Jesus feeds 5,000 and walks on water. | Jesus tries to be alone after hearing about John's death, but the crowds follow. He feeds them anyway.
Matthew 15 | Religious leaders from Jerusalem come to question Him. Then Jesus travels north to Tyre and Sidon. | The argument is about hand-washing traditions, not about germs. With the Canaanite woman, read to the end: her faith wins, and Jesus praises her.
Matthew 16 | Near Caesarea Philippi, far north. | Peter says Jesus is the Christ. Right after, Jesus starts telling them He must suffer and die. Christians read "this rock" in more than one way.
Matthew 17 | On a high mountain, then back down. | Three followers see Jesus shine bright, with Moses and Elijah. Down below, Jesus teaches about faith "as a grain of mustard seed."
Matthew 18 | In a house in Capernaum. The followers argue about who is greatest. | Jesus answers with a child, then teaches how to make things right with someone, and how often to forgive. Read verses 15 to 17 as steps toward healing a friendship.
Matthew 19 | On the road toward Jerusalem, in Judea. | Pharisees ask a trick question about divorce. A rich young man asks how to get eternal life. Jesus' answers are about the heart behind the question.
Matthew 20 | Still walking to Jerusalem. | The workers in the vineyard all get the same pay. The story is about grace, not about how to pay workers. Jesus says the greatest is the one who serves.
Matthew 21 | Jerusalem. Jesus rides in on a donkey, then clears the temple. | This is the last week of His life. The religious leaders question His authority, and He answers with parables aimed at them.
Matthew 22 | The temple courts in Jerusalem. Leaders try to trap Him with questions. | Each question is a trap: taxes, marriage after death, the greatest rule. Jesus answers that the greatest rules are to love God and love your neighbor.
Matthew 23 | The temple. Jesus speaks to the crowd and His followers about the religious leaders. | Seven times He says "Woe unto you." He is speaking to leaders who made faith heavy and showy. He ends by weeping over Jerusalem.
Matthew 24 | The Mount of Olives, looking at the temple. | His followers ask when the temple will fall and about the end of the age. Jesus answers both, and Christians read the timing in different ways. His main point: stay ready.
Matthew 25 | Still on the Mount of Olives. | Three stories about being ready: the ten young women, the money left to servants, and the sheep and goats. In the last one, how we treat the hungry and the stranger is how we treat Him.
Matthew 26 | Bethany, then the Last Supper, the garden, and His arrest. | Jesus shares the Passover meal and gives His followers bread and wine as His body and blood. In the garden He prays, "Not as I will, but as thou wilt."
Matthew 27 | Before Pilate, the cross, and the tomb. | On the cross, Jesus cries out the first line of Psalm 22. That Psalm starts in pain and ends in hope.
Matthew 28 | The empty tomb, then a mountain in Galilee. | Jesus is risen. He sends His followers to all nations and promises, "I am with you alway."
Mark 1 | The Jordan River, the wilderness, then Galilee. | Mark is the shortest Gospel and moves fast. Jesus' first message: "The kingdom of God is at hand: repent ye, and believe the gospel."
Mark 2 | Capernaum. A paralyzed man is let down through a roof. | Jesus forgives sins and heals, and the leaders are upset. "The sabbath was made for man" means the day of rest is a gift for people.
Mark 3 | A synagogue on the Sabbath, then a house. Jesus chooses the 12. | His family worries He has lost His mind. Leaders say His power comes from the devil. Jesus answers both.
Mark 4 | By the lake, teaching from a boat. Then a storm at night. | The parable of the Sower is the key to the others, Jesus says. Verses 11 and 12 quote Isaiah, and are about hearts that do not want to listen.
Mark 5 | Across the lake, then back. A man with many demons, a sick woman, and a dead girl. | Jesus crosses into non-Jewish land. Each person here was an outcast in some way, and He restores each one.
Mark 6 | His hometown of Nazareth, then He sends out the 12. Then the feeding of 5,000. | His hometown does not believe in Him. "A prophet is not without honour, but in his own country."
Mark 7 | Leaders from Jerusalem, then the region of Tyre. | The hand-washing argument is about traditions people added, not God's commands. With the Greek woman, her clever faith wins and her daughter is healed.
Mark 8 | Around the Sea of Galilee and north to Caesarea Philippi. | Peter calls Jesus the Christ. Jesus then teaches that following Him means taking up your cross.
Mark 9 | A high mountain, then down to a crowd. | After the shining on the mountain, a father cries, "Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief." Some hard sayings here are strong word-pictures, not instructions to hurt yourself.
Mark 10 | Judea, on the way to Jerusalem. | Children, a rich man, and blind Bartimaeus. Jesus came "not to be ministered unto, but to minister."
Mark 11 | Jerusalem: the donkey ride, the fig tree, and the temple. | The fig tree had leaves but no fruit, a picture of religion with no real life. Verses about faith moving mountains come right after.
Mark 12 | The temple courts. Trick questions and a poor widow. | The greatest commandments: love God, love your neighbor. The widow's two small coins were worth more than all the big gifts.
Mark 13 | The Mount of Olives, facing the temple. | Jesus talks about the temple falling and the end of the age. He says no one knows the day or hour. The point is to watch and be ready.
Mark 14 | Bethany, the Last Supper, Gethsemane, and the trial at night. | A woman pours costly oil on Jesus. He shares the bread and cup. He tells the high priest who He is.
Mark 15 | Before Pilate, the cross, and burial. | Jesus says very little at His trial. On the cross He quotes Psalm 22.
Mark 16 | The empty tomb. | The oldest copies of Mark end at verse 8. Verses 9 to 20 were added later, so many Bibles mark them. Read them with that in mind.
Luke 2 | The temple in Jerusalem. Jesus is 12 years old. | His first words in Luke: "Wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" His parents had been searching for three days.
Luke 4 | The wilderness, then His hometown synagogue in Nazareth. | Jesus reads from Isaiah: good news for the poor, freedom for captives. His hometown gets angry when He says God has helped outsiders too.
Luke 5 | The lake of Gennesaret (Sea of Galilee). | Peter has fished all night and caught nothing. Jesus says, "Launch out into the deep." Then Jesus calls a tax collector named Levi.
Luke 6 | Grain fields, a synagogue, then a level place. Luke's version of the sermon. | Luke's "Blessed are ye poor" goes with "Woe unto you that are rich." "Love your enemies" and "Judge not" are here too.
Luke 7 | Capernaum, Nain, and a Pharisee's dinner. | A Roman officer's faith, a widow's son raised, and a woman who washes Jesus' feet with tears. Those forgiven much, love much.
Luke 8 | Towns and villages, then across the lake. | Women who followed and supported Jesus are named. The Sower again: good ground is "an honest and good heart."
Luke 9 | Galilee, then Jesus turns toward Jerusalem. | Jesus sends the 12, feeds 5,000, and shines on the mountain. Near the end, short sayings show the cost of following Him.
Luke 10 | On the road. Jesus sends out 70 followers. | The Good Samaritan answers "Who is my neighbour?" Samaritans and Jews did not get along, so the hero was a surprise. Then Mary and Martha.
Luke 11 | Teaching on prayer, then a meal with a Pharisee. | Jesus teaches the Lord's Prayer and says to keep asking, seeking, and knocking. At dinner He warns leaders about being clean outside but not inside.
Luke 12 | A huge crowd. | Warnings about greed (the rich fool), and comfort about worry: "Consider the ravens." "Fear not, little flock."
Luke 13 | Teaching on the way to Jerusalem. | Bad things that happened to people were not punishment for being worse sinners. Jesus heals a bent-over woman on the Sabbath.
Luke 14 | Dinner at a Pharisee leader's house, then a large crowd. | Take the lowest seat. Invite people who can't pay you back. Verse 26 uses "hate" in a Hebrew way of saying "love less."
Luke 15 | Tax collectors and sinners come close. Pharisees grumble. | Three stories about lost things: a sheep, a coin, and a son. Each ends in a party. Do not miss the older brother at the end.
Luke 16 | Teaching His followers, with Pharisees listening. | The dishonest manager is a puzzling story. Jesus praises his cleverness, not his cheating. Then the rich man and Lazarus.
Luke 17 | On the way to Jerusalem, between Samaria and Galilee. | Ten men with leprosy are healed. Only one comes back to say thank you, a Samaritan.
Luke 18 | Still traveling. | Keep praying (the widow and the judge). Be humble (the Pharisee and the tax collector). Let the children come.
Luke 19 | Jericho, then Jerusalem. | Zacchaeus, a rich tax collector, changes his life. Jesus rides into Jerusalem and weeps over the city.
Luke 20 | The temple courts. Leaders question Him. | Trick questions about authority, taxes, and the afterlife. Jesus answers each one.
Luke 21 | The temple. | The widow gives two small coins. Then Jesus talks about the temple falling and about future troubles. Watch and pray.
Luke 22 | The Last Supper, the Mount of Olives, and His arrest. | Jesus shares the bread and cup. The followers argue about greatness, and He says to serve. The "two swords" line is often misread.
Luke 23 | Before Pilate and Herod, then the cross. | Jesus prays for the people crucifying Him: "Father, forgive them." He promises a criminal on the next cross, "Today shalt thou be with me in paradise."
Luke 24 | The empty tomb, the road to Emmaus, and Jerusalem. | Jesus walks with two followers who don't know Him. He explains the Scriptures, and they know Him when He breaks bread.
John 1 | The Jordan River, then Galilee. | John's Gospel starts before creation: "In the beginning was the Word." Jesus' first words here are a question: "What seek ye?"
John 2 | A wedding in Cana, then the temple. | At the wedding, Jesus turns water into wine. "Woman" was a polite word then, not rude.
John 3 | Night, in Jerusalem. Nicodemus, a Pharisee leader, comes to talk. | "Born again" can also mean "born from above." John 3:16 comes from this talk. Some think verses 16 to 21 are John's own words explaining it.
John 4 | A well in Samaria at noon. | Jews and Samaritans did not mix, and men did not talk to women alone in public. Jesus breaks both customs to talk to her about "living water."
John 5 | A pool in Jerusalem on the Sabbath. | Jesus heals a man who had been sick 38 years. The leaders are upset it happened on the Sabbath. Jesus explains that He works as His Father works.
John 6 | Across the Sea of Galilee, then the synagogue in Capernaum. | After feeding 5,000, Jesus says, "I am the bread of life." The "eat my flesh" words are a picture that shocked even His followers. Many left.
John 7 | Jerusalem at the Feast of Tabernacles, a fall harvest feast. | At this feast, water was poured out each day. So Jesus' words about "living water" landed with special meaning.
John 8 | The temple courts. | It opens with a woman caught in adultery. Then a long, sharp debate. "Ye are of your father the devil" was said to certain opponents in one argument, not to all Jewish people.
John 9 | Jerusalem. A man born blind. | The followers ask whose sin caused his blindness. Jesus says neither, and heals him.
John 10 | Jerusalem, then across the Jordan. | "I am the good shepherd." In verse 34 Jesus quotes Psalm 82, "Ye are gods," to answer leaders who called Him a blasphemer.
John 11 | Bethany, near Jerusalem. His friend Lazarus has died. | "Jesus wept," the shortest verse. Then He raises Lazarus and says, "I am the resurrection, and the life."
John 12 | Bethany, then Jerusalem, just before Passover. | Mary pours oil on His feet. Greeks come asking to see Him. Jesus speaks of the grain of wheat that must fall and die.
John 13 | The Last Supper. | Jesus washes His followers' feet, a servant's job. Then the new commandment: "love one another; as I have loved you."
John 14 | Still at the supper. | Jesus comforts His friends before His death: "Let not your heart be troubled." "I am the way, the truth, and the life" answers Thomas, who asked how to get where Jesus was going.
John 15 | Leaving the supper, maybe walking past vineyards. | "I am the vine, ye are the branches." Stay close to Him and you bear fruit. Verse 6 is a picture of a branch, not a threat.
John 16 | Still walking and talking at night. | Jesus promises the Comforter (the Holy Spirit). "In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer."
John 17 | Jesus prays out loud for His followers and for all who will believe. | This is the longest prayer of Jesus in the Bible. He prays that they "all may be one."
John 18 | The garden, then trials before the high priest and Pilate. | Jesus tells Pilate, "My kingdom is not of this world." Pilate asks, "What is truth?"
John 19 | The cross and the tomb. | From the cross, Jesus gives His mother into John's care. His last word here is "It is finished."
John 20 | The empty tomb and a locked room. | Mary Magdalene is the first to see the risen Jesus. Thomas doubts, then believes.
John 21 | The Sea of Tiberias (Galilee), at dawn. | Breakfast on the beach. Three times Jesus asks Peter, "Lovest thou me?" Peter had denied Him three times.
Acts 1 | The Mount of Olives, 40 days after the resurrection. | Jesus' last words before He goes up to heaven. He tells them to wait for the Holy Spirit and be His witnesses.
Acts 9 | On the road to Damascus. | The risen Jesus speaks to Saul (later called Paul), who was hurting His followers. Jesus says hurting them is hurting Him.
Acts 10 | Joppa. Peter has a vision. | The words here come in Peter's vision. The lesson is that God accepts people from every nation.
Acts 11 | Jerusalem. Peter retells his vision. | Peter repeats what he heard so others understand why he welcomed non-Jewish believers.
Acts 18 | Corinth, at night. | Jesus speaks to Paul in a vision: "Be not afraid, but speak." Paul stays there a year and a half.
Acts 20 | Miletus. Paul says goodbye to friends from Ephesus. | Paul quotes a saying of Jesus that is not in the four Gospels: "It is more blessed to give than to receive."
Acts 22 | Jerusalem. Paul tells his story to a crowd. | Paul retells his meeting with Jesus on the Damascus road.
Acts 23 | A Roman fort in Jerusalem, at night. | Jesus stands by Paul and tells him to be of good cheer. He will speak in Rome.
Acts 26 | Caesarea, before King Agrippa. | Paul tells his story a third time. Here Jesus adds, "It is hard for thee to kick against the pricks," like an ox kicking a farmer's stick.
1 Corinthians 11 | Paul writes to the church in Corinth. | Paul passes on Jesus' words at the Last Supper. The church was having meals where some ate a lot and the poor went hungry.
2 Corinthians 12 | Paul writes about his own weakness. | Paul asked three times to have a "thorn in the flesh" removed. Jesus answered, "My grace is sufficient for thee."
1 Timothy 5 | Paul writes to Timothy about church life. | "The labourer is worthy of his reward" is a saying of Jesus (Luke 10:7). Paul uses it to say teachers should be cared for.
Revelation 1 | The island of Patmos. John sees the risen Jesus. | Revelation is written in picture language, full of symbols. Jesus says, "Fear not; I am the first and the last."
Revelation 2 | Letters to four churches in what is now Turkey. | These were real churches in John's time. Each letter praises something, corrects something, and gives a promise.
Revelation 3 | Letters to three more churches. | Laodicea had hot springs nearby and cold water from another town, but its own water was lukewarm. "Behold, I stand at the door, and knock."
Revelation 16 | A vision of judgment. | In the middle of a scary scene, Jesus speaks: "Behold, I come as a thief." Revelation uses strong pictures to say evil will not win.
Revelation 22 | The last chapter of the Bible. | "Surely I come quickly." The warning about adding or taking away words is about the book of Revelation.
